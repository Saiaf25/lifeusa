2. Audience And Reader Need

The reader is a U.S. IRA owner age 70 1/2 or older, or a family member or advisor helping them research charitable giving. They need to distinguish a QCD from an ordinary withdrawal, understand the RMD relationship, confirm that the donor, account, and recipient qualify, coordinate the transfer, and preserve QCD-specific evidence.

Reader journey: I have an IRA -> I want to support a charity -> I check QCD eligibility -> I compare it with an ordinary withdrawal and gift -> I confirm current charity and custodian instructions -> I complete the direct transfer -> I preserve QCD-specific records.

Narrative bridge from the live cluster: The giving hub introduces IRA giving. The general tax guide explains that QCD tax treatment is separate from an ordinary charitable deduction. This article begins where those pages stop: the IRA-to-charity transaction itself.

7. Intro Guidance

Answer directly in 70 to 100 words. Use qualified charitable distribution naturally in the first sentence: a QCD is generally a distribution sent directly by an IRA trustee to an eligible charity for an IRA owner who has reached age 70 1/2. It may count toward an RMD, and the 2026 exclusion limit is indexed, but a withdrawal paid to the owner first is not the same process. Promise eligibility, RMD, steps, QCD-specific records, and mistakes.

Do not open with an appeal, a retirement recommendation, or “save taxes while giving.” Do not summarize general deduction rules, receipt thresholds, stock transfers, or donation apps in the introduction.

8. Detailed Outline

H2: What Is a Qualified Charitable Distribution?

  • Define QCD and spell out the acronym on first use.
  • Explain the direct trustee-to-eligible-charity route.
  • Distinguish a QCD from taking an IRA distribution personally and later donating cash.
  • State that a QCD is generally excluded from income when requirements are met; it is not also claimed as a charitable deduction.

Transition: The next question is whether the donor, account, and recipient qualify.

H2: Qualified Charitable Distribution Age: Who Is Eligible?

  • State the age rule carefully: the IRA owner must be at least 70 1/2 when the distribution is made.
  • Explain eligible IRA account types only to the depth supported by the current Publication 590-B.
  • Add a caution for ongoing SEP or SIMPLE IRA contributions and other edge cases; route to a tax professional rather than simplifying.
  • Keep inherited IRA discussion short and reviewed.

Use an eligibility card: age, eligible IRA, eligible recipient, direct transfer, annual limit, deadline.

H2: QCD vs. RMD: Can a Qualified Charitable Distribution Count Toward an RMD?

  • Define required minimum distribution (RMD) in one sentence.
  • State that QCD eligibility age and RMD starting age are not the same.
  • Explain that a qualifying QCD can count toward the current year's RMD if completed correctly and on time.
  • Do not provide a full RMD-age chart or individualized sequencing advice.

H3: Can You Send a Required Minimum Distribution to Charity?

  • Explain that the qualifying direct transfer may count toward the RMD; do not imply that every RMD payment is automatically a QCD.

H3: How Does an RMD to Charity Work?

  • Answer the short-form query in plain language and point back to the direct-transfer requirement.

H2: QCD Rules: Eligible Accounts, Direct Transfers, and Deadlines

  • Group the stable qualification requirements here: eligible owner, eligible IRA, direct payment, eligible recipient, and completion timing.
  • Keep annual-dollar thresholds in the following section so rules and limits are independently scannable.
  • Add a short reviewed caution that deductible IRA contributions made for years beginning at age 70 1/2 can reduce the amount of a later QCD that is excludable from income. Link to Publication 590-B and route calculations to a tax professional.
  • Do not repeat the full age explanation, QCD/RMD comparison, or annual limit here.

H2: Qualified Charitable Distribution Limit for 2026

  • State the indexed 2026 aggregate exclusion: $111,000 per eligible individual, subject to current rules.
  • Explain that married spouses do not share one pooled IRA limit; each person's eligibility and IRA matter.
  • Date-label the figure and cite IRS IRB 2025-49 and Publication 590-B.
  • Do not cover the one-time split-interest election, charitable trusts, or gift-annuity mechanics in Angela's draft. Those belong to advanced planned-giving guidance unless tax/legal review specifically requests a one-sentence boundary.
  • Explain that year-end completion, not merely starting a request, matters.

H2: How to Make a QCD from IRA Funds: A Six-Step Checklist

H3: 1. Confirm the donor and IRA are eligible

Check age on the distribution date, account type, current-year limit, and whether professional review is needed.

H3: 2. Confirm the charity is an eligible recipient

Use the IRS search and ask the charity whether it accepts QCDs. Do not assume every tax-exempt entity qualifies.

H3: 3. Request current charity and custodian instructions

Confirm the charity's legal name, tax ID, mailing or transfer route, donor-notification process, and any custodian-specific form. Do not copy old routing data from a blog post.

H3: 4. Instruct the IRA trustee to pay the charity directly

Emphasize the direct route. Explain that check procedures can vary, so the donor must follow the custodian's current QCD process.

H3: 5. Confirm completion before the deadline

Ask the custodian and charity to confirm when the transfer was completed and received. Start early; a submitted request is not proof of completion.

H3: 6. Keep the acknowledgment and tax records

Preserve the custodian confirmation, year-end tax form, charity acknowledgment, and correspondence identifying the gift. Give them to the tax professional. Keep QCD-specific proof here, then link to LIFE's donation-tracking guide for the general year-round organization system.

H2: Charitable Giving from IRA Funds: Which Charities Qualify?

  • Explain that the recipient must satisfy the current QCD rules, not merely use “nonprofit” in its name.
  • Link the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search.
  • Mention recipient exclusions only with current IRS wording and tax review.
  • State that supporting organizations, donor-advised funds, and certain private foundations need special attention; do not turn the section into an entity-law tutorial.
  • Do not repeat the giving hub's general charity-selection advice or imply that appearing in a nonprofit directory automatically establishes QCD eligibility.

H2: IRA Donation to Charity vs. a QCD

  • Explain that an ordinary IRA withdrawal followed by a separate donation is not the same transaction as a direct QCD.
  • Link general charitable-deduction questions to the canonical tax guide.
  • Avoid recommending one route for a specific reader.

H2: How Is an IRA Charitable Distribution Reported and Documented?

  • Explain at a high level that the IRA distribution is reported by the custodian and the taxpayer reports the qualifying amount appropriately on the return.
  • Mention Form 1099-R and the federal return only at a high level. Do not provide line-by-line form-filling instructions because forms and line numbers can change.
  • State that the charity acknowledgment should identify the organization and confirm whether goods or services were provided, without assigning the tax treatment.
  • Link to Publication 590-B, recommend professional review, and link to the donation-tracking guide only for general organization and backup practices.

H2: Common QCD Mistakes

  1. Taking the money personally before giving it to charity.
  2. Assuming QCD eligibility begins at the same age as RMDs.
  3. Sending funds to an ineligible recipient.
  4. Starting the transfer too late to complete in the intended tax year.
  5. Using a stale annual limit.
  6. Claiming a charitable deduction for the same excluded QCD amount.
  7. Failing to notify the charity who sent the custodian check.
  8. Treating a request confirmation as proof the charity received the funds.
  9. Ignoring the possible QCD adjustment for deductible IRA contributions made for years beginning at age 70 1/2.

H2: When a QCD May Not Be the Right Route

  • The donor has not reached the eligible age.
  • The gift is coming from a non-IRA account.
  • The intended recipient is not eligible.
  • The donor needs a different estate, trust, DAF, or retirement-planning strategy.
  • The custodian cannot complete the transfer before the deadline.
  • The donor expects goods or services in return, or another fact makes the transaction ineligible under current QCD rules.

Close with a professional-review recommendation, not a replacement strategy.
